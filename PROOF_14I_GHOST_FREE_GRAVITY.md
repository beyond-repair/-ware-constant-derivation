# Proof 14I — Ghost-free gravity models vs the W sector

**Date:** 2026-09-23. Gravity audit only. No 0.08. W is a dimensionless scalar in K, not a spin-2 field.

## Why gravity is on the table

12B allows ξ(W) M_Pl^2 R. T^{Cas} is a source in the Einstein equation if we couple ℓ_Cas. Higher-derivative or massive gravity changes the *left* side, not K. Ghost-freedom constrains that left side.

## Four standard classes

**Stelle quadratic gravity.** Finite R^2 + R_μν R^{μν} is renormalizable and has a massive spin-2 with the wrong-sign kinetic term (Weyl ghost) plus a healthy massive scalar. 4d quadratic gravity is not ghost-free.

**f(R).** Extra scalar only. Ghost-free if the scalar kinetic sign is healthy (Jordan/Einstein frame). Does not UV-complete graviton loops the way Stelle hoped. Identifying W with that scalar is a new postulate (W already sits in K).

**Infinite-derivative / entire-form-factor gravity (IDG).** Dress curvature terms with F(□/M^2) entire and zero-free ⇒ no extra poles; IR is GR; UV loops improve. Same logic as 14H for the graviton propagator. Form factor FREE. Does not determine W.

**dRGT massive gravity / ghost-free bimetric.** Tuned nonderivative potential; 5 healthy massive spin-2 dofs, no Boulware–Deser ghost. Bimetric: one massless + one massive spin-2. A closed-form infinite-derivative completion of a quadratic action can be rewritten as that bimetric theory; the series must be kept in full or the ghost returns.

New Massive Gravity is a 3d exception (no local massless graviton), not a 4d option.

## What this does *not* do for W

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| W is the massive graviton | NO (wrong spin, wrong slot) |
| W is the f(R) scalaron | POSTULATE, conflicts with K=m^2-Wλ unless identified by hand |
| ξ(W)R from IDG/dRGT | ξ still FREE |
| Ghost-free gravity selects W=0.08 | NO |
| T^{Cas} automatically conserved in IDG | only if the total action is diffs-invariant and the regulator is covariant (12D) |
| Replaces 14A missing J→W | NO |

Ghost-free gravity can be the left-hand side on which T^{Cas}+T^W lives. It does not generate the W-equation. Coupling a scalar W with ξ(W)R is Brans–Dicke-like; the extra scalar is healthy for a range of ξ, but that range is an inequality, not a value of W.
